# Thunder receiver geolocation has one informal precedent

As of 2026-10-20, only one source has located an audio recording's receiver by matching its thunder against a lightning catalogue: two YouTube videos by the OSINT creator **colsto** (August and September 2026). They describe essentially the same inversion as lightning-rs: a single-microphone low-band envelope, a position grid, a clock-offset scan, and, in the second video, the same MET Norway Frost catalogue. No paper, no code and no uncertainty quantification have been published for them ([video 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SnbkIm56TfI), [video 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6a7Z0I3LAnE)). The searches found no peer-reviewed paper, preprint or public repository that solves for a recorder's position or clock from thunder plus a catalogue. Academic thunder work (seismic "thunderquakes", fibre-optic DAS, infrasound arrays) runs the opposite way: it uses the lightning catalogue to identify or verify thunder at sensors whose positions and clocks are already known. The closest methodological relatives sit in other fields. Seismology recovers station clocks and positions from catalogued earthquakes and shots and has a mature geometry-based reliability theory (Bondár et al. 2004). Navigation solves for a receiver's position and clock from catalogued emitters (LEO signals of opportunity, pulsars). Media forensics fixes footage clocks from visible lightning flashes (El Reno, BAMS 2016), mains hum (ENF) or shadows. So the concept is no longer novel. A defensible proposal claims rigour instead: a calibrated likelihood, credible regions with validated coverage, a geometry-aware reliability flag, physical nuisance modelling, and a blind holdout. Neither colsto nor any academic analogue has combined those for this problem.

**How to read the verification labels.** "Full" means the full text was read. "Abstract" means only the abstract was read (via OpenAlex or the publisher page). "Snippet" means only a search-result extract or a fetch-tool paraphrase was seen. "Metadata" means only the bibliographic record was checked. "Self-report" means the claim rests on a video description, not a reviewed write-up. Items marked snippet or metadata should be re-verified before they are cited in a proposal.

## Two 2026 YouTube videos are the only end-to-end precedent

colsto's first video, **"Can you geolocate a video using background thunder sounds?"** (uploaded 2026-08-09, about 416k views), logged the Blitzortung/lightningmaps.org socket feed for roughly three weeks. For each candidate location it predicts an acoustic curve (slant-range delay with inverse-square attenuation) and scores it against the recording's **20–250 Hz RMS envelope by cosine similarity, using FFT cross-correlation over all time offsets** ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SnbkIm56TfI)). The description reports a blind check on a known location, a whole-world search with a known date but unknown time, and a search over a known region with about three weeks of time uncertainty. It also notes that commercial catalogues (Vaisala GLD360, ENTLN, Xweather) would improve per-strike accuracy. The second video, **"I located a fan's video using thunder sounds"** (2026-09-30, about 88k views), switched to the **MET Norway Frost API** ("~200 m vs ~3 km" for Blitzortung) and down-weighted the main strike cluster to favour outlying strikes. It reports a **2.2 km error, attributed to strikes clustered on one side of the listener**, and claims "~500 m should be feasible" with strikes from several directions ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6a7Z0I3LAnE)). The failure mode matches the lightning-rs simulation finding that locatability depends on how many strikes are audible and how they surround the receiver.

These results are **self-reports**. No code, paper or third video exists, and transcripts could not be retrieved because YouTube's caption endpoint returned nothing. Neither description mentions a likelihood, a credible region or a reliability criterion, but that absence cannot be confirmed without the transcripts. The method has drawn no independent replication. The only Hacker News submission got one point and no comments ([HN 49255716](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/items/49255716)), YouTube search shows no response videos, and Bellingcat and general OSINT guides do not mention thunder ([ShadowDragon](https://shadowdragon.io/resources/osint-techniques/)). colsto credits only "Benn Jordan's video on infrasound". The likely candidate, "Infrasound: What You Can't Hear CAN Hurt You" (2025-10-12), includes a distant-thunderstorm recording but no localisation method ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UTvr8L5v8u8)).

The GitHub repository and code searches found no public implementation (the code search may have been unauthenticated, so treat that result as weak). Repositories created since August 2026 go the other way. noah-zim/downstrike plays synthetic thunder from live Blitzortung strikes ([GitHub](https://github.com/noah-zim/downstrike/tree/main)). hitenjain22/lightning-project reconstructs lightning channels from simulated microphone-array thunder and uses no catalogue ([GitHub](https://github.com/hitenjain22/lightning-project)). Hobbyist tools are all forward models. LightningMaps draws sound rings expanding from each strike for a known listener ([webiano.digital](https://webiano.digital/lightningmaps-org-lets-you-watch-thunder-arrive/)), and Home Assistant users plot predicted thunder for their home location ([HA community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/blitzortung-showing-thunder-on-map/214560)). A Blitzortung forum user reports that "perhaps half the time" they hear thunder with no catalogued strike ([forum](https://forum.blitzortung.org/showthread.php?pid=10747)). This anecdotal detection-efficiency gap bears directly on a catalogue-driven inverse. The nearest conceptual precursor is a game exploit: Minecraft servers broadcast thunder-sound packets with true coordinates, which let players triangulate other players to about 500 blocks ([CurseForge plugin](https://dev.curseforge.com/projects/localized-thunder)). Access is also a practical barrier. Historical Blitzortung data with coordinates is restricted to contributors ([Blitzortung forum](https://forum.blitzortung.org/portal.php?page=4)), which pushes after-the-fact OSINT use towards Frost.

## Academic thunder research uses the catalogue to label, never to locate the sensor

Seismology, DAS and infrasound groups routinely associate thunder with NLDN, ENTLN, ALDIS, EUCLID, ATDnet, WWLLN or Blitzortung. In every item found, the catalogue serves as ground truth to identify events, verify array-derived source locations, label machine-learning training data, or correlate amplitude with peak current. The quantities inverted are near-surface velocity, source spectra and acoustic Q. **None solves for station position or clock.** This matters for the proposal because these groups own the ideal validation data (fixed sensors with known GPS positions and clocks plus years of catalogue-associated thunder) and have never turned the problem around.

The seismic line runs from Kappus & Vernon (1991), who recorded thunder on regional seismometers and took azimuth from three-component polarisation ([doi:10.1029/91JD00789](https://doi.org/10.1029/91jd00789)), through Lin & Langston's thunder-induced ground-motion papers, which grid-search near-surface layering ([doi:10.1029/2008JB005770](https://doi.org/10.1029/2008jb005770)), to the AlpArray study by **Novoselov et al. (2022, SRL)**. That study matches a large seismic dataset to ALDIS and finds that peak current correlates with maximum ground displacement for +CG flashes ([doi:10.1785/0220220064](https://doi.org/10.1785/0220220064)). Hong et al. (2022, GJI) identify thunder on a dense Seoul network only within **about 20 km** and find urban attenuation exceeds atmospheric absorption ([doi:10.1093/gji/ggac440](https://doi.org/10.1093/gji/ggac440)). That range independently supports the ~20–25 km audibility assumption in lightning-rs. The nearest academic gesture towards using lightning for sensor QC is Tytell et al. (2013, AMS), who ingested ENTLN into USArray processing to "optimize and quality-control seismic data". The summary does not describe recovering timing or position ([AMS](https://ams.confex.com/ams/93Annual/webprogram/Paper223194.html)).

DAS studies are over-determined by thousands of channels, so the catalogue serves only as a check. Zhu & Stensrud (2019) located thunder from a Penn State fibre and "verified with lightning locations from the National Lightning Detection Network" ([doi:10.1029/2019JD031453](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019jd031453)). **Roth et al. (2026, Science Advances)** manually picked 458 thunderquakes using NLDN strike times and locations, then used them as virtual sources for shear-velocity imaging to about 100 m depth. They state no explicit association rule and do no clock or channel calibration, and the paper is internally inconsistent about the recording period ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13496194/)). Roth et al. (2023) add a caution for any ground-sensor variant: coupled waves arise from scatterers near the receiver, which can offset apparent arrival times ([doi:10.1785/0120230116](https://doi.org/10.1785/0120230116)). The NORFOX array co-locates DAS, seismic and infrasound arrays with all-sky cameras, and its EGU26 abstract says lightning locations "provide independent timing and spatial context". That framing proposes a benchmark but demonstrates no calibration ([EGU26-17223](https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-egu26-17223); [PAGEOPH](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00024-026-04076-y)).

Infrasound arrays use a common association rule: **direct-path travel time plus a back-azimuth window**. BURARI in Romania accepts at most 10° deviation from ATDnet azimuth and associates reliably to 50 km ([EGU24-5622](https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-egu24-5622)). Assink et al. (2008) report the same 50 km limit in the Netherlands ([doi:10.1029/2008GL034193](https://doi.org/10.1029/2008gl034193)). CEEIN labelled 216,681 detections with lightning ground truth for an ML classifier (F1 0.877) ([doi:10.1029/2025JD044047](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025jd044047)). A single microphone has no azimuth term, and that term is the main disambiguator these studies rely on. Matching the envelope across many strikes at once replaces it. On the audio side, CNN thunder detectors exist (93% accuracy after combined filtering, [doi:10.3390/rs15020432](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15020432)), and crowdsourced smartphone work locates thunder from phones at known positions ([Sensors 2023, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10181301/)). Neither goes as far as associating a single microphone's thunder with a catalogue.

## Receiver-from-known-sources inverses mature in seismology and navigation

Treating catalogued sources as fixed and solving for the receiver is standard practice for seismic station metadata. Haase et al. (1995) found **12% of Southern California Seismic Network stations off by more than 500 m** from systematic travel-time residuals, later confirmed by GPS resurvey ([Caltech](https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechAUTHORS:20130222-080301036)). Syracuse et al. (2017) used catalogue residuals to flag month-long timing errors at 14 stations in and around Iran, and found two stations with wrong registry coordinates ([doi:10.1785/0220170113](https://doi.org/10.1785/0220170113)). Sukhovich et al. (2020) corrected hydrophone clock drift from teleseismic P arrivals ([HAL](https://hal.univ-brest.fr/hal-03040836/document)). A USGS study found station timing errors ranging from tens of milliseconds to over 10 s using repeating earthquakes ([USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70226473)). Ocean-bottom seismometers are relocated from active shots by grid search, which shifted 8 OBSs by a mean of 206 m ([Liu et al. 2022, doi:10.1007/s11001-022-09482-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11001-022-09482-0)). **US patent 8,995,222** solves jointly for OBS position and clock drift from first breaks of known shots. That is structurally the closest match to the lightning-rs joint position-plus-clock inverse, but it uses controlled sources and multiple observations per receiver ([USPTO](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8995222)). Ekström & Busby (2008) rotate seismometer components to fit predicted surface waves from catalogued earthquakes, and their QC page states that **estimates from fewer than 5 measurements are not reliable**. This is a count-based reliability flag that parallels the current lightning-rs flag ([IRIS PDF](https://ds.iris.edu/hq/files/programs/gsn/gsnqual/Ekstrom_Busby_2008_SRL.pdf); [QC page](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/~ekstrom/Projects/USARRAY/POLARIZATION/index2.html)). One negative result is worth noting: MERMAID floats do *not* locate themselves from catalogued earthquakes. They interpolate between GPS fixes, with mean mislocation of 99–685 m and heavier-than-Gaussian timing tails ([Nolet et al. 2024, doi:10.1785/0220230377](https://doi.org/10.1785/0220230377)). The Apollo lunar network also offers a cautionary sparse-geometry precedent. A seismically determined S-IVB impact site later proved to be **about 31 km** from the site found in LRO imagery ([LROC](https://www.lroc.asu.edu/images/364)).

The most transferable result is **Bondár, Myers, Engdahl & Bergman (2004, GJI)**. Formal linearised error ellipses assume Gaussian, uncorrelated errors and underestimate true uncertainty, "especially at high confidence levels". Accuracy is better predicted by network geometry. From Monte Carlo simulation of network geometry, local locations fall within 5 km at 95% confidence given **at least 10 stations within 250 km, an azimuthal gap under 110°, a secondary azimuthal gap under 160°, and one station within 30 km** ([doi:10.1111/j.1365-246X.2004.02070.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-246X.2004.02070.x)). The geometry is symmetric, so with strikes in place of stations these criteria can serve directly as a reliability rule for a receiver inverse. The grid-posterior machinery itself is standard in NonLinLoc (Lomax et al. 2000, [doi:10.1007/978-94-015-9536-0_5](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-015-9536-0_5)), though it is always applied to the source.

Navigation supplies the radio counterpart. Kozhaya & Kassas (2023) located a stationary receiver to **4.3 m** by blind Doppler tracking of 6 Starlink satellites over about 800 s, starting from a guess 200 km away ([doi:10.1109/VTC2023-Spring57618.2023.10199264](https://doi.org/10.1109/VTC2023-Spring57618.2023.10199264)). Follow-up work treats ephemeris error as a nuisance ([EuCAP 2026, doi:10.23919/EuCAP68105.2026.11612532](https://doi.org/10.23919/EuCAP68105.2026.11612532)), the analogue of catalogue position error. X-ray pulsar navigation suffers the same multimodality as a clock-offset scan, because phase ambiguity yields many candidate positions, and adding independent sources prunes them ([Lohan 2021](https://www.ideals.illinois.edu/items/120994)). Lightning-specific radio analogues are only patents: US Navy clock synchronisation from sferics across networked receivers, with no catalogue ([US9960901B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9960901)), and lightning-detector clock alignment from common events ([US4914444A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4914444)). Single-station sferic work locates the lightning, not the receiver ([Ann. Geophys. 2007](https://angeo.copernicus.org/articles/25/1509/2007/)). Microphone self-localisation without a catalogue needs several receivers and recovers only relative geometry ([El Badawy et al. 2021, arXiv:2102.03565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03565)). Infrasound uses catalogued rocket launches only to validate network detection: up to 73% of 1001 launches were detected at IMS arrays ([Pilger et al. 2021, doi:10.1029/2020GL092262](https://doi.org/10.1029/2020GL092262)). Even very large sources are therefore often missed, which parallels the audible-strike limit.

## Media forensics times footage by light, hum and shadow, not by sound

The peer-reviewed precedent for "media clock from a lightning catalogue" is **Seimon et al. (2016, BAMS), "Crowdsourcing the El Reno 2013 Tornado"**. It time-calibrated chaser video from 93 contributors by matching **intervals between visible CG flashes** to NLDN strokes above 20 kA, and discarded segments that could not be synchronised. Chaser positions came from Google Maps and Street View scene matching, not from lightning ([AMS](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/bams/97/11/bams-d-15-00174.1.xml); [Météo-France record](https://bibliotheque.meteo.fr/pub/DOC00038223-crowdsourcing-the-reno-2013-tornado-new-approach-f.html); [survey site](https://el-reno-survey.net/update-24-nov-13)). Interval matching is invariant to the clock offset, which makes it the optical, clock-only counterpart of the lightning-rs offset scan. The method details come from a search extract, because the BAMS full text returned 403. The 20 kA cut is a detection-efficiency guard: NLDN detects essentially all strokes at or above 20 kA but only about 80% of 5–20 kA strokes ([arXiv:2308.05662](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.05662)). A 2019 Tipton, Kansas tornado analysis reportedly used flashes the same way, but no team or paper could be identified ([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/weather/2019/06/17/this-beastly-tornado-left-behind-captivating-images-an-incredible-set-data/)).

Insurance and legal lightning forensics run as hypothesis tests rather than searches. Vaisala STRIKEnet (since 1992) lists every NLDN stroke within a radius, typically 10 miles, of a claimed loss location, each with its own confidence ellipse ([Warren Forensics](https://www.warrenforensics.com/2013/11/25/using-historical-weather-data-to-investigate-lightning-strike-claims/)). The Sago mine investigation (2006) cross-timed catalogued strokes against a seismically registered explosion. Sandia called them simultaneous, while MSHA's report put the strokes "one to five seconds before", so second-level timing gets contested in practice ([Sandia](https://newsreleases.sandia.gov/sandia-research-indicates-that-lightning-was-the-likely-cause-of-sago-mine-explosion/); [Mining Monthly](https://miningmonthly.com/markets/international-coal-news/1279637/msha-releases-sago-report)).

Physics-based analyses of crowdsourced footage exist for single known events, with camera positions known. Rigby et al. (2020) estimated the Beirut explosion yield at 0.50 kt from blast arrival times in 16 social-media videos ([Shock Waves](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00193-020-00970-z)). Borovička et al. (2013) derived the Chelyabinsk trajectory from casual videos ([Nature, doi:10.1038/nature12671](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v503y2013i7475d10.1038_nature12671.html)). Latour et al. (2025) estimated earthquake slip from a single CCTV camera, and its abstract does not describe how the camera clock was handled ([arXiv:2505.15461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15461)). Flash-to-bang tools give one range ring from one event ([sound-delay](https://github.com/davidnewschool/sound-delay)). Each inverts for the source, with known receivers. lightning-rs inverts for the receiver, with known sources.

The established forensic routes to "where and when from audio" are ENF and learned soundscapes. ENF matches mains-hum frequency against grid reference databases and works at grid scale, only coarsely within a grid ([Garg et al. ICASSP 2013](https://www.mast.umd.edu/files/paper_ENF/conf_paper6_ICASSP13.pdf); [arXiv:2105.00668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.00668)). Learned audio geolocation classifies cues such as species, bells or train horns ([arXiv:2505.18726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18726); IARPA [LocUS](https://www.iarpa.gov/research-programs/locus)). Sun-shadow chronolocation is the standard OSINT tool ([Bellingcat](https://www.bellingcat.com/resources/2021/05/18/unsure-when-a-video-or-photo-was-taken-how-to-tell-by-measuring-the-length-of-shadows/)). IMAGEGUARD reports a quantified verifier for it: 92.5% of true time/location claims accepted and 8.5% error on false ones ([arXiv:1811.08951](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.08951v1)). An "ADS-B as catalogue" microphone locator would share the thunder problem's structure, inverting closest-approach Doppler ([Martín et al., doi:10.1016/j.ast.2012.11.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ast.2012.11.011)) against known flight tracks, but no publication of it was found. Admissibility sets the bar. SWGDE's audio authentication best practices (v1.3, read in full) require peer-reviewed methods and measurement uncertainty for database-comparison analyses such as ENF. Conclusions must use a three-level scale (consistent, inconclusive or inconsistent) and must not rest on any single analysis ([SWGDE](https://www.swgde.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/2018-09-20-SWGDE-Best-Practices-for-Digital-Aud.pdf)).

## What the literature tells lightning-rs to build next

The geometry literature gives the clearest upgrade. The current reliability flag counts audible strikes, which corresponds to Ekström & Busby's under-5 rule. Bondár et al. show that count alone is insufficient and that the **azimuthal gap, secondary azimuthal gap (the gap left after dropping one source) and nearest-source distance** better predict true error. lightning-rs should compute these over the audible, catalogue-matched strikes and calibrate their thresholds by Monte Carlo on the simulation suite, as Bondár did with ground-truth explosions ([doi:10.1111/j.1365-246X.2004.02070.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-246X.2004.02070.x)). colsto's one-sided 2.2 km failure is exactly a large-azimuthal-gap case. A gap criterion turns his ad hoc outlier weighting into a principled abstention rule. Bondár's second finding, that formal Gaussian ellipses under-cover at high confidence, justifies reporting empirical coverage of the 90% region on held-out cases rather than trusting the nominal level. The heavy-tailed timing errors reported for MERMAID point the same way ([doi:10.1785/0220230377](https://doi.org/10.1785/0220230377)).

Validation data already exists in the fields that never inverted the problem. AlpArray with ALDIS, USArray TA with ENTLN, and the Penn State DAS fibre with NLDN all provide sensors with GPS-known positions and clocks and years of catalogue-associated thunder ([Novoselov 2022](https://doi.org/10.1785/0220220064); [Tytell 2013](https://ams.confex.com/ams/93Annual/webprogram/Paper223194.html); [Roth 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13496194/)). Pretending each station's position and clock are unknown gives a large blind benchmark with exact ground truth. Infrasound microphones co-located with these networks are the closest sensor match. NORFOX explicitly invites this use ([EGU26-17223](https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-egu26-17223)). The infrasound association literature also supplies concrete gates (direct-path travel time, a 50 km association ceiling for arrays, about 20 km for audible thunder at urban sites), and the CNN thunder detectors could pre-select segments. On the catalogue side, the El Reno >20 kA cut and the forum "thunder without strike" anecdote both argue for modelling **catalogue detection efficiency**, so that unexplained thunder is not counted as evidence against a candidate position.

Two cheaper extensions follow from the forensic material. First, report a SWGDE-shaped outcome: "consistent, inconclusive or inconsistent with claimed place X and time T", with the reliability flag deciding when to answer "inconclusive". That framing matches how STRIKEnet and ENF are actually used and how IMAGEGUARD reports accuracy. Second, the media notes raise an untested hypothesis. Audible sferic clicks (AM or EMI pickup within about 30 km, [US 7254484](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7254484)) arrive at effectively light speed, so matching a click train to catalogue stroke times would fix the clock independently of range. That would break the clock-range degeneracy thunder suffers from. The video flash clock already measured in this project is the optical version of the same idea. No prior art for sferic-in-audio chronolocation was found, so it remains a proposal-worthy idea, not an established method.

## Conclusion

The novelty verdict is split. Locating a single recorder by grid-scanning position and clock offset to match its thunder envelope against a lightning catalogue **is not novel**: colsto demonstrated it publicly in August–September 2026, on the same Frost catalogue, and reported the same geometry-limited failure. It **remains academically unpublished**. No paper or preprint was found, and colsto's work is unreviewed and code-free, has unverified accuracy figures, and has no stated uncertainty model. The proposal should cite colsto as concurrent informal work and claim a scientific contribution in what nobody has yet assembled: a calibrated likelihood with credible regions whose coverage is tested blind, a geometry-plus-count reliability flag grounded in Bondár-style criteria, physical nuisance terms (onset height, atmosphere), and benchmarking against permanent sensor networks with known ground truth.

Several gaps qualify the verdict. Most academic items were read only as abstracts or snippets. Chinese-language thunder-acoustics literature, Reddit and per-issue OSINT newsletters were not searched. The colsto transcripts, which could show whether he quantifies uncertainty, remain unread. The most valuable single verification before submission is to obtain those transcripts, because they decide whether "uncertainty-calibrated" is a genuine point of difference or a shared feature.
