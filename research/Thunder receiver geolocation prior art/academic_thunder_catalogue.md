# Academic work associating thunder at single/sparse sensors with lightning catalogues

Searched 2026-10-20 with OpenAlex API (abstracts via inverted index), Crossref API, WebSearch, and publisher pages. Wiley/AGU, MDPI and the Univ. Vienna thesis repository returned 403 to the fetcher, so most entries below are **abstract-only** (marked [abs]). [full] = full text read. [summary] = conference page paraphrased by the fetch tool, not quoted verbatim. Items already in research/prior_art_receiver_location.md and research/thunder_physics_lit.md (Few 1970, Anderson 2014, Rusz 2021, Farges 2021, Lacroix, Bestard, Arechiga, Johnson 2011, Gallin 2014 workshop, ThunderLoc, NORFOX EGU26 mention) are not re-reported except where a new detail was found.

## Seismic "thunderquakes" matched to lightning catalogues

### Takeaway
Seismic thunder is routinely matched to NLDN/ALDIS/ENTLN, but in every item found the catalogue is used to *identify* or *verify* thunder events and to correlate seismic amplitude with peak current; the only quantities inverted are near-surface (site) velocity structure and source spectra, never station position or clock. No paper found uses catalogued strikes as fixed sources to solve for a seismometer's location or timing.

### Cited Findings
- Kappus, M. E., & Vernon, F. L. (1991). Acoustic signature of thunder from seismic records. JGR Atmospheres. doi:10.1029/91JD00789 [abs]. Thunder triggered regional-earthquake seismometers (Kislovodsk, Karasu, USSR); peak frequencies 25–55 Hz at one station and 6–13 Hz at another; three-component polarization gives the azimuth to the bolt, and that azimuth changes during a record (horizontal channel). No catalogue association (pre-dates routine catalogues). A 1990 JASA abstract of the same work exists: doi:10.1121/1.2028860 — [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1029/91jd00789)
- Lin, T.-L., & Langston, C. A. (2007). Infrasound from thunder: A natural seismic source. GRL. doi:10.1029/2007GL030404 [abs]. 5 three-component surface seismometers + borehole + 5 infrasound mics; thunder excites near-surface P/S reverberations dependent on incident slowness and top-30 m velocity; proposed as a natural source for site-resonance hazard assessment. No catalogue use stated in abstract — [OpenAlex/Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1029/2007gl030404)
- Lin & Langston (2009). Thunder-induced ground motions: 1. Observations (doi:10.1029/2008JB005769) and 2. Site characterization (doi:10.1029/2008JB005770), JGR [abs]. Part 2 grid-searches near-surface layer thickness/velocities from thunder-induced motion plus air-coupled Rayleigh dispersion. Medium-property inversion from thunder, but source geometry comes from the co-located array slowness, not a catalogue — [Part 1](https://doi.org/10.1029/2008jb005769), [Part 2](https://doi.org/10.1029/2008jb005770)
- Lin & Langston (2006). Anomalous Acoustic Signals Recorded by the CERI Seismic Network. SRL 77(5):572–581. doi:10.1785/gssrl.77.5.572 [abs/landing page only; content not read] — [SSA](https://doi.org/10.1785/gssrl.77.5.572)
- Tytell, J. E., Reyes, J. C., Vernon, F., Sloop, C., Heckman, S., & Ali, A. (2013). "Utilizing Lightning Locations to Optimize and Quality-Control Seismic Data." 93rd AMS Annual Meeting, 6th Conf. on Meteorological Applications of Lightning Data, paper 6.6 [summary]. USArray Array Network Facility ingested near-real-time ENTLN feeds; combined with ~500 Transportable Array stations (seismic + barometric pressure at 40 sps) to process, review, verify and classify seismic/acoustic signals. The abstract (as summarised) mentions QC but not timing/position calibration. OpenAlex also lists an AGU FM 2013 abstract "Using the ENTLN lightning catalog to identify thunder signals in the USArray Transportable Array" by the same group (no DOI; not read) — [AMS](https://ams.confex.com/ams/93Annual/webprogram/Paper223194.html)
- Novoselov, A., Dorninger, M., Diendorfer, G., Bokelmann, G., & AlpArray Working Group (2022). Seismoacoustic Study of Thunder and Lightning Using the AlpArray. SRL. doi:10.1785/0220220064 [abs]. Large AlpArray seismic dataset matched to ALDIS (Austrian lightning location system), 10–49 Hz; asks whether flash information can be recovered from seismic data alone; finds a correlation between peak current and maximum thunder-induced ground displacement for +CG flashes. Association rule/travel-time model not in abstract — [OpenAlex/SSA](https://doi.org/10.1785/0220220064)
- Novoselov et al. (2021). ThunderSeis: Seismic analysis of thunder signals recorded at the Gaisberg mountain, Austria. EGU21-7606. doi:10.5194/egusphere-egu21-7606 [abs]. 5 thunder signals from tower-measured strokes (known position and current) on one-component seismic sensors, with a co-located met station used to model propagation precisely. Known-source/known-receiver forward modelling, not inversion — [Copernicus](https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-egu21-7606)
- Novoselov, A., Fuchs, F., & Bokelmann, G. (2020). Acoustic-to-seismic ground coupling: coupling efficiency and inferring near-surface properties. GJI. doi:10.1093/gji/ggaa304 [abs]. Firecracker/rocket sources (not thunder): ~2% of acoustic energy enters the ground; Lamé constants and Poisson ratio (0.336–0.366) inferred from the direct airwave. Methodological basis for the AlpArray thunder work — [OUP](https://doi.org/10.1093/gji/ggaa304)
- Novoselov (2019) CTBT S&T conference poster "ThunderSeis: Seismic analysis of thunder infrasound" [summary]: proposes ALDIS + AlpArray as a multiyear dataset for systematic comparison and suggests thunder may shed light on lowest-troposphere processes. A 2021 Univ. Vienna dissertation ("ThunderSeis") exists but its PDF could not be retrieved — [CTBTO](https://conferences.ctbto.org/event/17/contributions/3442/)
- Hong, T.-K., Park, S.-J., Chung, D., & Kim, B. (2022). Inversion of acoustic thunder source spectral model from thunder-induced seismic waves in megacity. GJI. doi:10.1093/gji/ggac440 [abs]. Dense Seoul network, 4 thunder events; signals identified at <~20 km; direct acoustic-to-seismic waves travel at ~340 m/s apparent velocity; atmospheric acoustic Q determined; urban landscape causes strong attenuation beyond atmospheric absorption; source spectra peak 34–36 Hz (~4×10^6 J/m) — [OUP](https://doi.org/10.1093/gji/ggac440)
- Lythgoe, K., Loasby, A., Hidayat, D., & Wei, S. (2021). Seismic event detection in urban Singapore using a nodal array…: earthquakes, blasts and thunderquakes. GJI. doi:10.1093/gji/ggab135 [abs]. Thunder is a false-positive class for local tectonic detection — [OUP](https://doi.org/10.1093/gji/ggab135)
- Voss, Dahl-Jensen & Larsen (2015). Acoustic events on a small seismological network – shock waves from thunder and fireballs. GEUS Bulletin 33. doi:10.34194/geusb.v33.4481 [abs]. Thunder flagged to keep the Danish earthquake database clean — [GEUS](https://doi.org/10.34194/geusb.v33.4481)
- Díaz et al. (2023). Monitoring storm evolution using a high-density seismic network. Sci. Rep. doi:10.1038/s41598-023-28902-8 [abs]. 1.5 km station spacing, Cerdanya; seismic amplitude >40 Hz tracks rainfall; thunderstorms analysed — [Nature](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-28902-8)

### Inferences
- Seismic thunder literature treats the catalogue as ground truth for event identification and source strength, exactly the forward association step this repo uses, but nobody has turned it around to estimate station timing or position. The nearest is Tytell et al. 2013 ("quality-control seismic data"), which is conference-only and does not (as summarised) claim timing/location recovery.
- Hong et al. 2022's "~20 km" identification range and strong urban attenuation is consistent with the repo's ~20–25 km audibility assumption, from an independent sensor type.

### Gaps
- Novoselov 2022 SRL full text (association windows, sound speed, distance statistics) not read: paywalled and thesis PDF unretrievable.
- Tytell AGU FM 2013 abstract not located online; could not confirm whether arrival-time residuals vs ENTLN were used to flag station timing problems.

## DAS recording thunder matched to lightning catalogues

### Takeaway
DAS thunder studies (Penn State FORESEE, Hefei, triggered lightning) locate thunder sources from the fibre and *verify* against NLDN or local lightning data; they invert near-surface phase velocity and, in Roth et al. 2026, shear-wave structure to ~100 m. None calibrates fibre channel positions or timing with catalogued lightning; NORFOX only proposes lightning as independent timing/spatial context.

### Cited Findings
- Zhu, T., & Stensrud, D. J. (2019). Characterizing Thunder-Induced Ground Motions Using Fiber-Optic Distributed Acoustic Sensing Array. JGR Atmospheres. doi:10.1029/2019JD031453 [abs]. 18 thunder events in 30 min (15 Apr 2019); peak frequencies 20–130 Hz; arrival times used to estimate near-surface phase velocity, back azimuth and source location, "verified with lightning locations from the National Lightning Detection Network"; time-reversal migration; DAS thunder power positively correlated with NLDN current power — [OpenAlex/Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019jd031453)
- Hone, S., & Zhu, T. (2021). Seismic Observations of Four Thunderstorms Using an Underground Fiber-Optic Array. SRL. doi:10.1785/0220200264 [abs]. 4.9 km Penn State fibre; time-domain grid search for back azimuth/slowness; modified Geiger's method for source location; reconstructs storm movement and measures seismic velocity — [SSA](https://doi.org/10.1785/0220200264)
- Roth, N., Zhu, T., Czarny, R., & Gao, Y. (2023). Wavefield Modeling and Analysis of Lightning Quakes Measured by a DAS Array. BSSA. doi:10.1785/0120230116 [abs]. Lightning quakes start as airborne acoustic waves and couple into air-coupled Rayleigh and Love waves generated by local scatterers (topography, infrastructure) near the receiver; electroseismic coupling negligible; Rayleigh/Love phase velocities estimated — [SSA](https://doi.org/10.1785/0120230116)
- Roth, N., Kim, D., Czarny, R., Kim, Y. C., Wauthier, C., & Zhu, T. (2026). Imaging Earth's subsurface with thunderstorm-generated seismic waves. Science Advances 12(34):eaeg8096. doi:10.1126/sciadv.aeg8096 [full, via PMC]. 4 km fibre, 2,137 channels at 2 m, 250 Hz; 458 "high-fidelity thunderquakes" identified **manually** using a library of NLDN strike times/locations; arrival times "align well with estimated air-wave travel times calculated from the source parameters of the NLDN"; no explicit association rule, sound speed or distance range given; thunderquakes used as virtual sources for Vs tomography to ~100 m (Geopsy Dinver). No clock or channel-position calibration. Paper is internally inconsistent on recording period (2019–2021 vs 2019–2022) and virtual-shot count (~141,000 vs >170,000) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13496194/)
- Hong, H., Wang, B., Lu, G., Li, X., Ge, Q., & Xie, A. (2024). Tracking Lightning Through 3D Thunder Source Location With Distributed Acoustic Sensing. JGR Atmospheres. doi:10.1029/2023JD038882 [abs]. 7.7 km Hefei telecom cable as 3,850 sensors; 101 thunder events manually identified from six +CG flashes in 20 min; signals dominated by direct acoustic waves, not air-ground coupled surface waves; locations and amplitudes "generally consistent" with the conventional lightning detection dataset; thunder sources span ground to ~5 km — [OpenAlex/Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1029/2023jd038882)
- Hong, H., Tian, Y., Wang, B., Lu, G., Lyu, W., & Fan, Y. (2025). Diagnostic analysis of triggered lightning with distributed acoustic sensing. npj Clim. Atmos. Sci. doi:10.1038/s41612-025-01261-z [abs]. 4.2 km cable (2,100 sensors) around a triggering site; near-ground location accuracy ~30 m vs high-speed video; thunder mainly from return strokes rather than leaders — [Nature](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41612-025-01261-z)
- Turquet, A., Wüestefeld, A., Baird, A. F., Iranpour, K., Dichiarante, A. M., Stokkan, S. (2026). NORFOX: An Experimental Fibre-Optic DAS Array for Seismo-Acoustic Monitoring in Norway. Pure Appl. Geophys. doi:10.1007/s00024-026-04076-y [abs]. Co-located with NORES seismic and infrasound arrays; all-sky camera and weather station record lightning to support interpretation; abstract does not describe lightning-based calibration — [Springer](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00024-026-04076-y)
- Turquet et al. (2026). Reimagining Seismic Array Processing with Fibre-Optic DAS: The NORFOX Array. EGU26-17223 [abs]. New detail beyond the earlier note: the lightning reference comes from Norsk Meteor Nettverk all-sky cameras, and "Lightning locations provide independent timing and spatial context" for interpreting acoustic signatures; framed as a benchmark dataset for DAS calibration, not a demonstrated calibration — [Copernicus](https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-egu26-17223)

### Inferences
- In DAS work the catalogue is a check on array-derived locations; because the array geometry (thousands of channels) over-determines source location, there is no need to trust the catalogue as a fixed source. A single microphone has no such redundancy, so the repo inverts the opposite direction.
- Roth 2023's finding that coupled waves are generated by local scatterers near the receiver is a caution for any seismometer/DAS analogue of this project: apparent arrival times can be offset by local conversion, not just air path.

### Gaps
- No paper found that calibrates DAS channel positions or timing using lightning/thunder. Vehicle-based channel geolocation (SRL 2023, already in prior file) remains the closest analogue.
- Full texts of Zhu & Stensrud 2019 and Hong 2024 (exact NLDN matching tolerance, sound speed used) not read (403).

## Infrasound stations associating detections with lightning catalogues

### Takeaway
Single-array infrasound studies (KNMI Netherlands, IS17 Ivory Coast, BURARI Romania, PSZI Hungary, CEEIN, Czech microbarometers) associate detections to EUCLID/WWLLN/ATDnet/Blitzortung/national networks using a direct-path travel time plus a back-azimuth tolerance; reliable direct association reaches ~50 km for regional arrays, with thunder detectable to hundreds of km via stratospheric ducts at IMS stations. Catalogue association is used for classification and for training ML labels, not for inversion.

### Cited Findings
- Assink, J. D., Evers, L. G., Holleman, I., & Paulssen, H. (2008). Characterization of infrasound from lightning. GRL. doi:10.1029/2008GL034193 [abs]. Netherlands; correlation of infrasound detections with an EM lightning detection network "successful up to distances of 50 km"; CG blastwave signals 1–5 Hz; amplitude partly explained by a line-source beam pattern at 3.9 Hz up to 20 km — [OpenAlex/Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1029/2008gl034193)
- Farges, T., & Blanc, E. (2010). Characteristics of infrasound from lightning and sprites near thunderstorm areas. JGR. doi:10.1029/2009JA014700 [abs]. Eurosprite 2005 mini-array in France; lightning <100 km; 3-D inversion of source locations; amplitude vs distance; 0.01–10 Hz — [OpenAlex/Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1029/2009ja014700)
- Earlier Ivory Coast (IS17) statistics reported by search snippet only, not verified against a primary text: ~300 thunderstorms, >16,000 infrasound events matched one-to-one to WWLLN; ~60% of WWLLN flashes within 20 km matched; up to 25% of flashes detectable acoustically overall — [ResearchGate figure record](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Distribution-of-thunder-infrasound-amplitude-in-comparison-with-the-lightning-distance_fig2_258467682) (unverified; treat as approximate)
- Farges, T., Hupe, P., Le Pichon, A., Ceranna, L., Listowski, C., & Diawara, A. (2021). Atmosphere 12(9):1188. doi:10.3390/atmos12091188 [abs] — already known; new detail from abstract: most 0.5–5 Hz detections at IS17 are thunder even for storms up to 500 km away, and stratospheric detections' annual azimuth pattern tracks the equatorial stratospheric semi-annual oscillation — [MDPI](https://doi.org/10.3390/atmos12091188)
- Ghica, D. V., & Antonescu, B. (2024). Infrasound from lightning measured in northern Romania. EGU24-5622. doi:10.5194/egusphere-egu24-5622 [abs]. BURARI array vs Met Office ATDnet, summers 2020–2022, 0.5–7 Hz. **Association rule stated:** a relationship between infrasound time-of-arrival and discharge time under a direct-path assumption, plus a maximum 10° deviation between observed back-azimuth and ATDnet back-azimuth; successful to 50 km; dominant frequency ~3 Hz, amplitudes 0.01–0.5 Pa — [Copernicus](https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-egu24-5622)
- Pásztor, M. S., Czanik, C., & Bondár, I. (2020, 2021). EGU2020-446 (doi:10.5194/egusphere-egu2020-446) and EGU21-6525 (doi:10.5194/egusphere-egu21-6525) [abs]. Blitzortung used as ground truth, correlated with PMCC detections at Hungary's PSZI array (operating since May 2017) to identify/track storms and test detection direction, distance and accuracy; seasonal direction/distance variation analysed. Also a Hungarian-language Légkör 2024 paper (doi:10.56474/legkor.2024.2.1) — [EGU2020](https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-egu2020-446), [EGU21](https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-egu21-6525)
- Pásztor, M. S., Šindelářová, T., Ghica, D. V., Mitterbauer, U., Liashchuk, O., Lacanna, G., et al. (2025). Automatic Signal Discrimination Using Machine Learning on the Data From the Central and Eastern European Infrasound Network. JGR Atmospheres. doi:10.1029/2025JD044047 [abs]. 216,681 labelled detections; thunderstorm class labelled using lightning data as ground truth; ensemble F1 0.877 (0.906 after transfer learning to a new station) — [Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025jd044047)
- Chum, J., Diendorfer, G., Šindelářová, T., Baše, J., & Hruška, F. (2013). Infrasound pulses from lightning and electrostatic field changes: Observation and discussion. JGR Atmospheres. doi:10.1002/jgrd.50805 [abs]. Narrow 1–2 s pulses 11–50 s after electrostatic field changes; distance/azimuth vs EUCLID suggest most selected events are IC discharges; high elevation angles. Predecessor of Rusz 2021 — [Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgrd.50805)
- Haney, M. M., Van Eaton, A. R., Lyons, J. J., Kramer, R. L., Fee, D., & Iezzi, A. M. (2018). Volcanic Thunder From Explosive Eruptions at Bogoslof Volcano, Alaska. GRL. doi:10.1002/2017GL076911 [abs]. Single microphone array at 60 km; thunder back-azimuth consistent with lightning-network locations; for 8 Mar 2017, arrival times and amplitudes of high-frequency thunder "correlate well with the timing and strength of lightning detections." Follow-up: Haney et al. (2020), Bull. Volcanol., doi:10.1007/s00445-019-1349-y (no abstract retrieved) — [Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017gl076911)
- Lamb, O. D., Lees, J. M., & Bowman, D. C. (2018). Detecting Lightning Infrasound Using a High-Altitude Balloon. GRL. doi:10.1029/2018GL078401 [abs]. Stratospheric balloon over Tasman Sea recorded infrasound from lightning strokes within 100 km — [Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018gl078401)
- Nippress, A., & Green, D. N. (2023). Global empirical models for infrasonic celerity and backazimuth. GJI. doi:10.1093/gji/ggad334 [abs]. Not thunder-specific; empirical celerity/back-azimuth deviation models for association of automatic detections — relevant as the generic infrasound association machinery — [OUP](https://doi.org/10.1093/gji/ggad334)

### Inferences
- The common association rule across these is: direct-path travel time (fixed celerity/sound speed) + back-azimuth window (e.g. ±10° at BURARI; distance-dependent at IS17). Single-microphone work cannot use the azimuth term, which removes the main disambiguator these studies rely on; the repo's envelope-over-many-strikes match replaces it.
- The repeated ~50 km direct-association limit is for 0.5–7 Hz infrasound on arrays; audible-band single-mic reach is shorter (~20–25 km per thunder_physics_lit.md).

### Gaps
- Exact celerity/time windows used by Assink 2008 and Farges 2021 not obtained (full texts blocked).
- No Raspberry Shake & Boom peer-reviewed study associating thunder with a catalogue was found; only forum anecdotes (already in prior file).

## Single-station audio thunder detection and citizen science matched to strike lists

### Takeaway
Automated audio thunder detection exists (CNN/MFCC classifiers, combined filtering), and ThunderLoc crowdsources phones, but no peer-reviewed study found matches single-microphone audio thunder automatically to a lightning catalogue's strike list.

### Cited Findings
- Wang, Y., Yang, J., Zhang, Q., Zeng, J., Mu, B., & Du, J. (2023). Application of Combined Filtering in Thunder Recognition. Remote Sensing 15(2):432. doi:10.3390/rs15020432 [abs]. Spectral subtraction + low-pass before a CNN raises thunder-vs-noise accuracy to 93.18% (+3.8–18.6%); sub-band variance endpoint detection; motivated by short-range lightning location — [MDPI](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15020432)
- Zhang, H., Yan, B., Gu, S., An, C., Li, J., & Wu, M. (2021). Thunder Signal Detection via Deep Learning. J. Phys.: Conf. Ser. 1828:012023. doi:10.1088/1742-6596/1828/1/012023 [abs, partial]. MFCC-based 3-channel tensor into a deep network — [IOP](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-6596/1828/1/012023)
- Wilson, T. C., Petrin, C. E., & Elbing, B. R. (2023). Infrasound and Low-Audible Acoustic Detections from a Long-Term Microphone Array Deployment in Oklahoma. Remote Sensing 15(5):1455. doi:10.3390/rs15051455 [abs]. 6-year 0.1–200 Hz three-mic array, public data; abstract lists many verified sources but not lightning/thunder catalogue matching — [MDPI](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15051455)

### Inferences
- A labelled thunder detector (Wang 2023 style) could gate which audio segments feed the envelope match, but the literature offers no catalogue-association benchmark for single microphones.

### Gaps
- Searches for "crowdsourced" + "thunder" + "lightning detection network" returned only ThunderLoc (already known) and unrelated crowdsourcing reviews.

## Using catalogued lightning acoustics to invert atmospheric or medium properties

### Takeaway
Inversions using thunder from catalogued strikes target the *ground* (near-surface velocity, Lamé parameters, site resonance) and the *source* (spectra, energy), plus atmospheric acoustic attenuation (Q). The only atmospheric-dynamics inference is statistical (IS17 stratospheric azimuth vs the semi-annual oscillation). No study found inverts tropospheric wind/temperature profiles from catalogued thunder travel times.

### Cited Findings
- Near-surface structure from thunder: Lin & Langston 2009 Part 2; Zhu & Stensrud 2019 (phase velocity); Hone & Zhu 2021 (seismic velocity); Roth et al. 2023 (Rayleigh/Love phase velocity); Roth et al. 2026 (Vs to ~100 m from 458 NLDN-verified thunderquakes) — sources as cited above.
- Atmospheric attenuation: Hong, T.-K. et al. 2022 determined acoustic Q and found urban-landscape attenuation exceeds atmospheric absorption — [OUP](https://doi.org/10.1093/gji/ggac440)
- Stratospheric dynamics: Farges et al. 2021 — azimuth of stratospheric thunder detections follows the semi-annual oscillation (statistical, not a profile inversion) — [MDPI](https://doi.org/10.3390/atmos12091188)
- Propagation-error modelling for thunder location: Guo, J., Wang, C., Jia, X., Song, J., Yang, H., & Sun, Z. (2024). Study and Analysis of the Thunder Source Location Error Based on Acoustic Ray-Tracing. Remote Sensing 16(21):4000. doi:10.3390/rs16214000 [abs]. Ray tracing without wind: straight-line model gives the largest error, temperature stratification next, humidity negligible; error varies >80% with source height; low sources at long range give "very large" errors — [MDPI](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs16214000)
- Wind effect at short range (controlled explosions, not lightning): Silber, E. A., Bowman, D., Egan, S., Burkett, L., Fleigle, M., & Kim, K. (2026). Observational Evidence for Wind-Driven Low-Pass Filtering of Infrasound at Short Range. GRL. doi:10.1029/2025GL120042 [abs]. 31 stations within 23 km of 10-t TNT-equivalent shots; upwind paths show systematically longer periods (wind low-pass filtering) and azimuthal bifurcation in period and celerity — [Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025gl120042)

### Inferences
- Guo 2024 independently supports the repo's finding that low-source/long-range geometry dominates propagation error (cf. thunder_physics_lit.md refraction-shadow table).
- Silber 2026 implies azimuth-dependent celerity and envelope smearing within ~20 km, relevant to the repo's wind-shear-by-azimuth nuisance idea, even though it is not thunder.

### Gaps
- No paper found using known catalogue strike positions/times to fit effective sound speed or wind from thunder arrivals at one station. This analogue inversion appears unpublished.

## Who has proposed or attempted locating a receiver from thunder

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed paper or preprint found proposes or attempts locating an unknown recorder from thunder plus a lightning catalogue. The closest academic statements are (a) Tytell et al. 2013 using ENTLN to quality-control USArray data, and (b) NORFOX 2026 proposing lightning as an independent timing/spatial reference for DAS; neither solves for receiver position or clock. This confirms the verdict in research/prior_art_receiver_location.md (only colsto's 2026 YouTube videos do the full inversion).

### Cited Findings
- Tytell et al. 2013 AMS — [AMS](https://ams.confex.com/ams/93Annual/webprogram/Paper223194.html)
- Turquet et al. EGU26-17223 — [Copernicus](https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-egu26-17223)
- WebSearch for thunder-based seismometer orientation, clock or station location from lightning catalogues returned no matching work (only conventional orientation-error studies and lightning-location patents) — search 2026-10-20.
- OpenAlex searches "thunder lightning network clock offset estimation acoustic sensor" and "geolocation audio recording thunder lightning" returned nothing relevant (top 8 each).

### Inferences
- Every ingredient exists separately in peer-reviewed form: catalogue-to-sensor association (all sections above), receiver-structure inversion from thunder (near-surface), and seismic station clock/position recovery from catalogued earthquakes (prior file). The specific combination — catalogue strikes as fixed sources, single sensor, solve for sensor position + clock — is unpublished academically as of 2026-10-20.
- Seismic networks with ALDIS/ENTLN/NLDN overlays (AlpArray, USArray TA, Penn State DAS) would be a natural validation set for the repo's method: known station positions/clocks, years of catalogue-associated thunder.

### Gaps
- Semantic Scholar was not queried this session (OpenAlex used instead); Google Scholar not queried. A non-English (Chinese) literature check was not done; the Hefei/CAMS groups are the most active thunder-acoustics groups and could have relevant Chinese-language work.
